# How to work the revised prospect list

Start with the current master workbook. The original 50 are retained as the user's preferred baseline, with their original IDs and research dates. Additions require a documented commercial format, a relevant drink or recipe application, and a public business contact route. This is a prospect list; no buyer has agreed to buy.

## What changed

The earlier expansion gave too much weight to proximity and broad cafe / dessert categories. A shake counter, delivery listing, fruit bowl, or generic coffee menu does not establish a reason to buy dehydrated fruit. The revision removes those weak matches and records a reason for every retained or excluded expansion row. Closed venues and unresolved operating-status conflicts are excluded. Multi-outlet names and hotel room counts are not treated as purchase-volume evidence.

The stronger formats are established cocktail bars, brewpubs with mixed drinks, dine-in cafes with specific coolers or tea recipes, production bakeries with a relevant ingredient trial, and hotels with an identifiable beverage programme. Some smaller premises can still qualify through a strong recipe and buyer route. Large hotels can involve slower purchasing approval, central contracts and payment terms; brand size is not a conversion forecast.

## The order to approach them

1. **Local beverage trials:** call the public business line during a quiet service period, ask for the bar captain or beverage manager, and identify one current drink. Request a short appointment before visiting from Poranki.
2. **Local hotel accounts:** get the F&B manager's interest in a specific recipe first. Then ask purchasing what supplier documents, pack specifications, delivery schedule and payment terms they require. A reservations email is only a routing contact.
3. **Bakery and chocolate accounts:** speak to the production or pastry chef. Establish whether they need ordinary dehydrated fruit, candied fruit, puree or freeze-dried ingredients. These are different products; a small hot-air dehydrator does not produce all of them.
4. **Hyderabad and farther cities:** qualify by phone / email first. Obtain a named recipient and agreement on the trial before paying for courier delivery. Coordinate sister outlets and shared buying routes.

The workbook's priority score is a transparent sorting aid based on evidence, format, contact access and geography. It is not a percentage chance of conversion. Purchasing friction remains a separate field so a polished hotel website does not hide a difficult supplier route. Read the row's evidence and qualification gap before spending on a sample.

## A specific first conversation

“Hi, I'm [name] from Poranki, setting up a small dehydrated-fruit supply business. Could I speak to the person who decides your drink garnishes? I saw [verified drink / programme]. Do you currently buy the garnish or prepare it in-house? If dried citrus could suit one drink, may we agree a small orange or lemon sample comparison?”

For each interested buyer, record the current garnish, accepted fruit and diameter, usable pieces per pack, weekly consumption reported by the buyer, acceptable delivered cost per piece, approval process and next step. Keep quantities blank until someone provides them. Ask whether they already dry fruit internally and what would justify changing suppliers.

## The sample and follow-up

Offer orange and lemon wheels first, with lime kept as a separate specification. Add pineapple only for a drink or recipe that the buyer approves. A menu containing orange juice is a reason to ask about garnish, not evidence that dried orange is already wanted.

Agree the sample quantity, any charge, delivery details and assessment date. Ask the bartender to compare appearance, aroma, breakage, stability in the drink and usable cost against their current method. Send only product and storage information you have established for your actual process.

After the agreed tasting date, ask for a small paid trial with a clear specification. Follow up once if there is no response, then pause or use the buyer's requested date. Record refusals and stop requests. A positive tasting is not a repeat customer: the first paid reorder is the stronger signal.

## Analytics that matter after outreach

Track the funnel by sales route and geography: attempted contacts, conversations with a decision maker, recipe accepted, sample agreed, sample accepted, paid trials and reorders. Calculate rates only from actual counts and display their denominators. Public menu research cannot supply a credible conversion percentage or monthly demand estimate.

Compare accepted pieces and delivered contribution after rejects, packaging and courier cost. Use the workbook's Pilot Costing sheet with measured batch data. Prioritize fruits and accounts that reorder, rather than building inventory for the number of prospects in the sheet.

## Evidence limits

“Reviewed” means a public page or labelled search-index result was inspected. Contacts were not called, inboxes were not tested, and operating status can change. Index-only contacts need a fresh check before travel or sample spending. Original-50 records are preserved and clearly distinguished from additions assessed under the new criteria. The Sources and Changes sheets provide the audit trail; historical files live only under archive.
